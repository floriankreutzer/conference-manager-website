# Conference Manager Website — Content Governance

## 1. Purpose

Public website copy is a product artifact. Claims must be accurate, current, reviewable, and consistent with the implemented Conference Manager product and approved positioning.

Marketing language may simplify; it may not invent.

## 2. Source hierarchy

Use this precedence when public statements conflict:

1. current implemented behavior and security boundaries in the authoritative product repositories;
2. current approved Conference Manager architecture/product documentation;
3. current approved Confluence Product Positioning & Brand Playbook;
4. brand-specific approved messaging/homepage application pages;
5. campaign or page-specific draft copy.

If marketing copy conflicts with implementation, correct or qualify the marketing copy. Do not change product truth to preserve a slogan.

## 3. Canonical product truths

Current strategic baseline:
- official product name: **Conference Manager**;
- category: **Conference Management Software for Workplace Teams**;
- core claim: **One request. Everything your conference needs.**;
- key differentiator: **Keep your room booking. Replace the coordination around it.**;
- existing room-booking systems remain in place and are connected rather than replaced;
- Microsoft 365 / Entra are important initial ecosystem integrations, not the primary category/brand proposition;
- initial commercial focus is DACH while the product is international by design.

## 4. Corporate-brand status

Current state:
- PAVUREL — preferred corporate-brand candidate, not legally cleared;
- SAVELUN — reserve candidate, not legally cleared.

Until professional clearance and explicit adoption:
- do not claim PAVUREL is registered, trademarked, legally adopted, or already the company name;
- do not state ownership of domains that have not been acquired/verified;
- do not imply legal clearance through copyright/trademark symbols without basis;
- keep `Conference Manager` as the product name;
- use `by Pavurel` only as the approved subordinate candidate endorsement in contexts where that status is acceptable.

## 5. Claim classification

Every material capability/security/integration claim should be treated as one of:

### Verified current
Supported by current authoritative implementation/documentation and suitable for public wording.

### Qualified current
Available only under explicit conditions, pilot/tenant configuration, environment, or integration status. Public wording must include the material qualification.

### Target state
Planned or designed but not currently delivered. Do not present as live capability. Use on public roadmap material only when an explicit roadmap publication decision exists.

### Candidate / unvalidated
Brand, market, messaging, or customer-language hypothesis not yet externally validated. Do not present evidence of market preference or proven results.

### Prohibited / unsupported
No evidence or intentionally out of scope. Do not publish.

## 6. Capability verification

Before publishing concrete feature language:
- verify current behavior in `conference-manager` / `conference-manager-api` and current product documentation;
- confirm whether the feature is available in production, demo, pilot, or only target-state;
- verify role/tenant/security limitations;
- verify relevant localization and environment availability;
- remove or qualify claims that cannot be proven.

Screenshots must pass the same verification. A visually plausible mockup is not proof of implemented functionality.

## 7. Microsoft and integration language

Where implementation is verified, preferred high-level wording:

**Works with Microsoft 365.**

Do not:
- make Microsoft the hero/category of the product;
- describe Conference Manager as a Microsoft 365 Conference Management Platform unless strategy explicitly changes;
- imply all Microsoft services are integrated;
- imply integrations are universally available if tenant/admin setup is required.

Future integrations must not be presented as current.

## 8. Room-booking language

Maintain the approved differentiation:

**We don't replace your room booking system. We connect it.**

Do not imply Conference Manager is the native system of record for room inventory/availability unless current implementation and architecture explicitly support that statement.

## 9. Security, privacy, and compliance claims

Security/trust content must be especially conservative.

Do not publish:
- certifications that have not been achieved;
- claims of formal compliance without an evidence basis;
- absolute terms such as `100% secure`;
- unsupported data-residency statements;
- unsupported encryption, retention, backup, availability/SLA, penetration-test, or audit claims.

Differentiate design goals, implemented controls, contractual commitments, and external certifications.

## 10. Customer proof

Never invent or imply:
- customer names/logos;
- testimonials;
- case studies;
- ratings/reviews;
- user counts;
- revenue/adoption metrics;
- time saved;
- ROI or productivity percentages.

Customer proof requires explicit permission and a verifiable evidence source.

## 11. Competitive/superiority claims

Avoid unsupported absolute superiority terms such as:
- `best`;
- `leading`;
- `#1`;
- `only`;
- `most advanced`;
- `proven`.

Comparisons must be factually supportable, current, and reviewed for legal/market risk.

## 12. Homepage message hierarchy

Public homepage messaging should prioritize:
1. conference-management outcome;
2. employee and Workplace Team value;
3. one-request model;
4. room-booking integration rather than replacement;
5. hospitality/brand character;
6. connected ecosystem;
7. business/security readiness;
8. conversion.

Do not open with architecture, SSO, Microsoft, governance, or a long feature catalogue.

## 13. Conversion actions

Primary marketing conversion: **Book a demo**.  
Secondary: **See how it works**.  
Utility/application action: **Login / Sign in**.

`Login` must be clearly distinguishable from demo/contact conversion and must send existing users to the Conference Manager application.

## 14. Localization governance

English is the canonical baseline; German must be a complete localized customer experience.

Translation should preserve meaning and product truth rather than mechanically mirror word order.

Do not publish mixed-language pages, placeholder translations, or untranslated metadata/accessibility text in production.

## 15. Review triggers

Re-verify affected public claims when any of these change:
- relevant product milestone completed;
- feature added/removed/feature-flagged;
- integration availability changes;
- security architecture changes;
- corporate-brand legal status changes;
- pricing/commercial model changes;
- deployment/data-hosting model changes;
- customer evidence becomes available;
- relevant public page is materially rewritten.

## 16. Publication gate

Before production publication of a page:
- factual claims verified;
- target-state items removed/qualified;
- brand/legal status checked;
- translations complete;
- screenshots verified;
- links/CTAs work;
- SEO metadata accurate;
- security/privacy statements reviewed;
- no invented social proof;
- no placeholder content.
